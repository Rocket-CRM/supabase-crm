# Standard Rocket company profile and differentiation

## Purpose

This resource supplies approved company-level material for proposal writers. It is a selection source, not a paragraph to paste in full. Each proposal must use only the facts and differentiators that help the buyer assess Rocket for that specific engagement.

Run-specific TOR facts, legal qualifications, past-work values, named personnel, and implementation commitments still come from the run's `sources/` and supporting evidence.

## Approved company facts

Use these facts in an official, evidence-led tone:

- **Company:** Rocket Innovation Co., Ltd. / บริษัท ร็อคเก็ต อินโนเวชั่น จำกัด.
- **Positioning:** One of Thailand's leading specialist providers of CRM and customer-engagement software.
- **Recognition:** Ranked among the top three providers in multiple relevant categories in the **2025 MarTech Report**. The proposal run must record the exact report title, categories, pages, and evidence asset before making a more specific ranking claim.
- **Customer scale:** More than **100 corporate and enterprise clients**.
- **Technical capacity:** More than **30 technical staff**.
- **Specialisms:** CRM; B2C and B2B customer service software; loyalty; marketing automation; and governed AI-assisted customer operations.
- **Industry leadership:** Rocket personnel contribute to Thailand's CRM and marketing-technology community through industry presentations and practical demonstrations of customer-data, loyalty, automation, and AI use cases.

Do not write "Thailand's number-one CRM" unless the run contains evidence that supports that exact ranking and category. "One of Thailand's leading CRM specialists" is the approved default because it is consistent with a top-three ranking.

## Differentiation library

### 1. Configurable depth without one-off structural cost

Rocket combines a configurable product foundation with project-specific design and development. Reusable CRM objects, rules, workflows, permissions, and integration patterns can be composed into a solution that reflects the buyer's operating model without treating every requirement as a separate custom subsystem.

Use this when the project values:

- configurable workflows, forms, stages, rules, roles, or service levels;
- a hybrid product-plus-custom delivery model;
- future policy changes that should not require rebuilding the platform; or
- tailored behavior with lower long-term structural and maintenance cost than a one-off custom build.

For a loyalty proposal, examples may include earning, progression, redemption, engagement, and activation. For another domain, rewrite the examples using that buyer's own objects and journeys.

### 2. Modern, integration-ready architecture

Rocket designs customer platforms around clear application boundaries, unified APIs, durable multi-step workflows, auditable state changes, and processing patterns proportionate to the expected load and hosting environment.

Use this only after adapting it to the project's technical-practices context. A buyer-installed government system must not inherit claims about Rocket-operated cloud, multi-tenancy, message brokers, SIEM, or 24/7 infrastructure operations unless the TOR and dossier support them.

### 3. Governed AI capability

Rocket develops AI-assisted customer-service and customer-engagement capabilities that work from governed customer context, permissions, knowledge, and measurable actions.

Selection rule:

- If AI is required or materially relevant, explain the specific in-scope use case and controls.
- If AI is not required but company breadth is useful, one restrained company-profile sentence is acceptable.
- Do not present AI as part of the proposed implementation, architecture, price, or delivery plan unless the source pack or human-approved scope includes it.
- Omit AI when it distracts from the evaluation criteria.

## Proposal selection procedure

1. Read the bidder criteria and scoring factors before writing the company introduction.
2. Record approved facts and evidence in the run's `sources/`.
3. Select no more than three company messages that directly support the buyer's decision.
4. Rewrite those messages in the buyer's vocabulary and official proposal tone.
5. Keep legal qualification evidence, past-work contracts, and personnel proof in their required forms or appendices.
6. Add a short evidence map when it helps evaluators find the supporting documents.
7. Remove any claim that conflicts with the proposed hosting model, delivery scope, or implementation commitments.

## Recommended company-introduction shape

Use a compact section rather than a corporate brochure:

1. **Company profile** — specialist category, scale, and relevant disciplines.
2. **Suitability for this engagement** — project-specific reasons Rocket's operating model and capacity fit.
3. **Relevant differentiation** — normally configurable depth and architecture; AI only when selected by the rule above.
4. **Supporting evidence** — point to the required corporate, past-work, recognition, and personnel documents.
5. **Optional proof visual** — one relevant recognition or industry-leadership image with a factual caption.

The company introduction summarizes suitability; it does not replace the bidder-qualification documents.

## Evidence checklist

Before final submission, verify the run contains or identifies:

- company registration and financial-qualification documents required by the bid;
- 2025 MarTech Report extract with exact categories and ranking;
- client-count evidence approved for external use;
- technical-headcount evidence approved for external use;
- qualifying past-work contracts and completion certificates;
- signed personnel forms, degree certificates, and experience evidence; and
- source and caption for any industry-event image.

If evidence is pending, record the gap in the dossier. Do not strengthen the wording to compensate for missing proof.
